The gap

SEC 10-K and 10-Q filings contain rich forward-looking language — supply chain pressure, pricing power, competitive threats — that moves markets. But it's unstructured, verbose, and impossible to read at scale. Off-the-shelf sentiment tools flatten this nuance to a single positive/negative score.

The question

Can a language model trained specifically on financial MD&A extract the nuanced, factor-level sentiment that actually predicts stock returns — and can reinforcement learning align that model to real market outcomes?

Each stage is versioned and auditable — the pipeline skips completed steps on resume, every output is committed as an artifact, and the backtest grid was pre-registered before observing results.

The model reads each subsection of an MD&A filing and answers 60 targeted questions. For each question it finds evidence for, it produces a directional summary and verbatim quote. Here's what that looks like on a real filing passage:

Input · filing excerpt (MD&A, supply chain section)
"Supply chain disruptions continued to impact component availability throughout the fiscal year. We experienced lead times extending from 12 to 26 weeks on key electronic components, resulting in delayed deliveries to certain OEM customers and increased procurement costs. Management is actively diversifying supplier relationships and investing in safety stock to mitigate forward risk, though near-term margin pressure is expected to persist."

One passage → three distinct factor signals. The model scores them independently, capturing that the current situation is negative but management is acting — a nuance a single sentiment score would collapse.

Each factor receives one of five ordinal labels. The model was fine-tuned specifically to distinguish tail signals — the very negative and very positive cases that the long-short strategy actually trades.

After SFT, the model classifies language well — but "well" by Opus's annotation rubric, not by market outcomes. GRPO reinforcement learning closes that gap by directly optimizing for what matters: does the sentiment predict the stock return?

Policy (SFT model)

Reward signal
Composite reward vs realized 21-day return quintile
Composite reward function
Ordinal credit
Distance-0 → 1.00
Distance-1 → 0.50
Distance-4 → −1.00
Tail-miss penalty
−0.30 if gold is polar (very neg/pos) and model predicts neutral
Format gate
R = −1.0 if JSON fails to parse; enforces output structure
Why GRPO over PPO or DPO? PPO requires a critic network (too large for 14B on one A100). DPO needs pairwise preferences (we have ordinal quintile labels, not pairs). GRPO consumes scalar rewards natively and its multi-sample rollout doubles as the Best-of-N verifier at inference time.

Why IR 2.02 matters: An Information Ratio above 1.0 is considered strong by institutional standards. This hit 2.02 on data the model had never seen — the real test. The signal also survived 5–10 bps trading costs with 85%+ IR retention at 3 and 6-month horizons.
01
Each pipeline stage contributed measurably. Base → SFT → GRPO is strictly monotone on L/S spread (+2.78% → +4.88% → +8.09%). Every component earns its compute.
02
The signal is a tail detector, not a linear factor. Rank-IC ≈ 0 across all variants — middle cohorts carry near-zero predictive mass. Only extreme sentiment predicts returns.
03
Defense sector shows the strongest lift (+18.2% at SFT). Language in defense filings is highly predictive — specific procurement language, contract backlog signals.
04
Transports sector has inverted sentiment. Upbeat airline filings predict mean-reversion. Distress-rebound dynamics dominate — a production system should exclude or gate this sub-sector.
05
Longer horizons are more cost-resilient. At 25 bps costs, H=21 loses 76% of IR. H=63 and H=126 retain 58% and 34% respectively — the signal is designed for patient capital.
06
OOS performance matched or exceeded IS in 11/12 scenarios. This is the strongest evidence the model learned something real, not a pattern that happened to fit the training window.
01
Pre-register before you look at results. The entire 48-cell backtest grid was locked before test inference ran. This is the only way to know a result isn't p-hacked — and it forced cleaner thinking upfront.
02
Leakage hides in unexpected places. Mid-project audit found IC weights computed on the full panel (including test years) flowing into signal aggregation. The fix was simple — the detection was hard. Always trace data lineage end-to-end.
03
GRPO's lift is portfolio-level, not sample-level. Sample F1 barely moved. But the shape of the predictive distribution shifted — more decisive tails — which compounded through aggregation into large portfolio gains. Evaluation metrics need to match the actual use case.
04
Honest failure disclosure builds more trust than polished results. The CORN verifier failed completely (PMF collapsed to constant mean). Documenting it clearly — including why it failed and what was learned — is more valuable than hiding it.
05
Sector-specific behavior matters more than aggregate metrics. The aggregate signal looked strong. But transports was inverted, defense was dominant, general industrials was the only sector with a clean monotone ladder. Production deployment requires sector-aware rules.
